The Lore

The Tenth Picture

There is an old sequence of ten pictures about taming an animal that refuses to sit still. Nine of them happen on the mountain. The tenth happens in the market.

I

The Monkey Mind

The old teachers had a word for it: kapicitta, the monkey mind. It swings from branch to branch and never once chooses a branch — thought to thought, want to want, and every new noise turns its head. Monk was born with the worst case in the forest. He reached for the ripe fruit and then for the unripe, for what he already held and for what he did not, and woke every morning poorer than he had gone to sleep.

II

The Rope

The cure, they told him, is a rope. Not to strangle the animal — the old instruction is clear about that, and almost everyone gets it wrong — but to hold the other end and follow it uphill for as long as it takes. The rope is attention. The animal is you. Nine days of stone that gave nothing back, and at the summit no fruit, no treasure, nobody waiting — only a flat rock, warm from a sun he had never once stopped to notice.

III

The Whitening

In the old pictures the creature turns white a little at a time, from the face backwards, as the one holding the rope stops fighting it. Nothing is driven out. It is fed, walked, sat beside, and one day it lies down of its own accord. Rain came. Winter came. Traders came, shouting about the price of things, and left the way they had arrived. Look at his face in any of these images. That part went first.

IV

The Two Travellers

Every telling of this story sends two down the road: the quiet one who knows where he is going, and the loud one with the staff who can do anything except sit still. Neither of them arrives without the other. Monk was only ever given one body to carry them both, and a name that is half of what he was and half of what he became. He came down the mountain exactly once — to write a single line on the ledger of a chain that never sleeps, for everyone still swinging.

The tenth picture is the one where he walks back into the market. His line in it is $monk.
